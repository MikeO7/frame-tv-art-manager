Frame TV Art Manager vs. SmartThings for Samsung Frame TV art

Short answer: if you add a photo to your Samsung Frame TV now and then, Samsung’s SmartThings app is simpler. Frame TV Art Manager is a SmartThings alternative for people who already run a NAS or home server and want the TV to follow a folder automatically, with several TVs, large collections, and automatic rotation when the TV is full. You can also use both.

This page only compares ways of getting your own pictures onto The Frame’s Art Mode. Frame TV Art Manager is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Samsung. It uses Samsung’s private Art Mode protocol and targets Frame TVs running Tizen 8.0 or newer.

When should I choose Frame TV Art Manager?

  • You already run a NAS, home server, mini PC, or Mac that stays on.
  • Your art lives in a folder, or you want it to.
  • Your collection is bigger than the TV can hold and you want it to rotate automatically.
  • You have more than one Frame TV.
  • You shoot lots of portrait photos and want them prepared consistently.
  • You’d like art from NASA, museums, or photo sites to arrive on its own.

When is SmartThings the better choice?

  • You only add a photo now and then.
  • You don’t have, or don’t want, a computer running all the time.
  • You prefer an official Samsung app with Samsung support.
  • You’d rather pick each photo by hand on your phone.
  • You use other SmartThings features anyway.

Can I use SmartThings and Frame TV Art Manager together?

Yes. Frame TV Art Manager only manages the pieces it uploaded. Anything you add through SmartThings stays on the TV and is never rotated out or cleaned up, unless you deliberately set REMOVE_UNKNOWN_IMAGES=true.

Art you add yourself still takes up space on the TV. If you fill the TV mostly with your own pieces, rotation has less room to work with.

Does Frame TV Art Manager replace SmartThings?

No. Frame TV Art Manager keeps your own art in sync and can optionally set brightness, the slideshow, a nightly power-off, and Wake-on-LAN. It isn’t a remote control, a phone app, a SmartThings replacement, or an official Samsung client.

It also doesn’t choose which piece is on screen. That stays with the TV and its slideshow.

Why not just use a simple upload script?

A script that uploads files is easy. Keeping a TV matched to a folder for months without mistakes is harder. You need to know which pieces on the TV you uploaded and which you didn’t, avoid treating a failed folder scan or an unreachable TV as “delete everything”, cope with a full TV, and survive a power cut halfway through saving.

Frame TV Art Manager keeps checksummed records of what it uploaded to each TV, saves them so a crash can’t leave a half-written file, and makes no changes when it can’t confirm what’s on the TV.

What is the main tradeoff?

Frame TV Art Manager asks for some setup and a machine that stays on. In return, keeping the TV’s art fresh becomes a matter of changing a folder. SmartThings needs no server and is Samsung’s supported route, but every change is done by hand.
