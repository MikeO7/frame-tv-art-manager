How to send Apple Photos to a Samsung Frame TV

Short answer: turn on Frame TV Art Manager’s upload page, then send photos to it from your iPhone, iPad, or Mac. The quickest way is the upload page in Safari; an iPhone Shortcut or a Mac script can send your favorites or an album on a schedule. Uploaded photos join the artwork folder and reach your Samsung Frame TV on the next sync, within 30 minutes by default.

This guide is for people who choose favorites or keep albums in Apple Photos but run Frame TV Art Manager on a NAS or home server. Photos travel straight from your device to your server over your home network. Whether your Photos library itself uses iCloud is up to your Apple settings; Frame TV Art Manager doesn’t touch it.

How does sending Apple Photos to the Frame TV work?

  1. Your iPhone, iPad, or Mac sends a JPEG or PNG to http://SERVER-IP:8080/upload, signed in with a password you choose.
  2. Frame TV Art Manager checks that it’s a real, complete image within the size limit and saves it in the artwork folder under a generated name. Sending the same file twice stores it once.
  3. On the next sync, 30 minutes by default, the photo is prepared for the 3840×2160 screen and uploaded to each of your Frame TVs.

SERVER-IP throughout this guide means the address of the machine running Frame TV Art Manager, not the TV.

Quickest: the upload page in Safari

On your iPhone, iPad, or Mac, open http://SERVER-IP:8080/upload in Safari and sign in. Tap the drop area, choose one or more photos, and the page sends them one by one, showing each result. On a Mac you can also drag files onto it.

The page only accepts JPEG and PNG. iOS normally converts HEIC photos to JPEG when a page asks for those formats, but if a photo is rejected, use the Shortcut below, which converts explicitly.

How do I build an iPhone or iPad Shortcut?

A Shortcut can send your Favorites, or an album, in one tap or on a schedule. First, make the sign-in value the Shortcut needs. On a Mac or Linux computer, run this with your own token and copy the output:

Then build the Shortcut:

  1. Create the shortcut. Open the Shortcuts app, tap +, and name the shortcut “Send to Frame TV”.
  2. Find the photos. Add Find Photos. Filter for favorites or an album, add a filter for Media Type is Image so videos are skipped, and turn on Limit with a small number while you test.
  3. Loop over them. Add Repeat with Each and pass it the photos.
  4. Convert to JPEG. Inside the loop, add Convert Image and convert Repeat Item to JPEG. iPhone photos are usually HEIC, which the uploader does not accept.
  5. Upload each photo. Still inside the loop, add Get Contents of URL. Set the URL to http://SERVER-IP:8080/upload and the method to POST. Set Request Body to Form and add a File field named file containing the converted image. Under Headers, add Authorization with the value Basic followed by a space and your encoded credentials.
  6. Test it. Tap the play button. Each photo should come back with File imported successfully, or File already exists (deduplicated) if it was sent before.

Apple sometimes renames these actions and options between iOS versions. If a label doesn’t match, look for the same idea: find photos, convert each to JPEG, and POST it with the Authorization header.

Run it on a schedule

Once the Shortcut works when you tap it, open the Automation tab, tap +, choose Time of Day, pick a time such as 2:00 AM daily, choose to run it immediately rather than asking first, and select “Send to Frame TV”. Whether iOS lets it run without a confirmation depends on your iOS version.

Each run re-sends the photos it finds. Identical files are recognized and skipped, but if iOS produces a slightly different JPEG for the same photo on a later run, that copy is stored as a new picture. Keep the album or favorites list focused, or use the Limit setting, to keep things tidy.

How do I send favorites from a Mac automatically?

On a Mac, the free third-party tool osxphotos can export your favorites or an album as JPEGs, and a short script uploads them.

  1. Give your terminal app access to the Photos library: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, then turn on Terminal (or iTerm).
  2. Install osxphotos, for example with pipx install osxphotos.
  3. Save this script as ~/frame-tv-sync.sh, fill in your server address and token, and run chmod +x ~/frame-tv-sync.sh.

To send an album instead of favorites, replace --favorite with --album "Frame TV". Because the export folder is kept between runs, --update only exports new photos, and files already uploaded are recognized as duplicates.

What happens to a photo after it’s uploaded?

On the next sync, Frame TV Art Manager prepares it like any other picture: turns it upright, applies your portrait setting (crop by default, or pad or collage), converts color to sRGB, sharpens lightly, and resizes it to exactly 3840×2160. Then it uploads it to every TV in TV_IPS, with your MATTE_STYLE if you set one.

Uploaded photos stay in the artwork folder until you delete them. To take one off the TV, delete its file from the folder (the upload response tells you its name); the next sync removes it from the TV. If the TV is full, uploaded photos join the rotation like everything else.

What do the upload errors mean?

Responses from /upload
ResponseMeaning and fix
401 Authentication requiredWrong username or password. The username is always frame; check the Base64 value in your Shortcut.
403 Upload endpoint is disabledSet UPLOAD_ENABLED=true and restart. Uploads are also unavailable while DRY_RUN=true.
400 file too largeThe photo is over MAX_DOWNLOAD_SIZE_MB (20 MB by default). Raise the limit or send a smaller export.
400 Invalid or unsafe imageNot a complete JPEG or PNG. HEIC photos land here; convert to JPEG first.
400 missing 'file' parameterThe form field isn’t named file.
429 Another artwork import is in progressTwo uploads were sent at once. Send them one after another.

Is the uploader secure?

  • It’s off until you set UPLOAD_ENABLED=true, and every request needs the password.
  • Plain HTTP doesn’t encrypt the password or your photos. Keep the uploader on your home network, or put a TLS reverse proxy in front of it.
  • Never forward port 8080 from your router to the internet.
  • Uploaded files are checked to be complete images before they’re saved, and your original filenames are never used on the server.
  • Uploaded photos take up space on the server until you delete them.
