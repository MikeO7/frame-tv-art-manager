Automation guide

How to automatically upload art to a Samsung Frame TV

Short answer: run Frame TV Art Manager on a NAS or home server, give it your Frame TV’s IP address, and put JPEG or PNG files in its artwork folder. It uploads new pictures to the TV automatically at startup and then every 30 minutes, removes ones you delete from the folder, and leaves art you added yourself alone. No SmartThings or Samsung account is needed.

Samsung’s SmartThings app is fine for sending the odd photo to The Frame. It gets tedious when your art changes often, when several TVs should show the same collection, or when your pictures already live on a NAS. Frame TV Art Manager replaces those repeated uploads with a folder that the TV follows.

What does “automatic” mean here?

Frame TV Art Manager treats a folder as the list of art that should be on your Samsung Frame TV. At startup and every 30 minutes after, it compares the folder with what’s stored on the TV, uploads anything missing, and removes pieces it uploaded that you’ve since deleted from the folder. You never open an app; you just change the folder.

What do I need to automate Frame TV art uploads?

  • A Samsung Frame TV running Tizen 8.0 or newer, connected to your home network. Other firmware may work, but it isn’t the target.
  • An always-on machine on the same network that can run Docker or a Go binary: a NAS, Unraid server, mini PC, Raspberry Pi-class board, or Mac.
  • A fixed IP address for each TV, set with a DHCP reservation in your router.
  • A folder that survives restarts, for your art and the TV’s pairing token.
  • Pictures in JPEG (.jpg, .jpeg) or PNG (.png) format.

Setup takes one setting, TV_IPS. The Docker setup guide walks through it.

How does automatic Frame TV art sync work?

Each sync runs the same four steps.

1. The folder decides what belongs on the TV

Every JPEG and PNG in the artwork folder (/data/artwork in Docker) is part of your collection. You can fill it however you like: copy files over a network share from your computer, use the upload page or an iPhone Shortcut, or let it download art from online sources. Exact duplicates are recognized by their contents and stored once.

Hidden files, temporary files, JSON and YAML files, and symlinks are ignored. Control files such as mattes.json are never uploaded as art.

2. Each picture is prepared for the screen

Before uploading, Frame TV Art Manager makes each picture fit the Frame’s 4K panel: it turns sideways photos upright using their EXIF orientation, crops or pads portraits, converts color profiles to sRGB, tone-maps HDR PNGs, applies light sharpening, and resizes to exactly 3840×2160. JPEGs are saved at quality 95. A typical 24-megapixel photo takes about one to two seconds.

The prepared version replaces the original in the artwork folder and may get a new name, so copy pictures in rather than moving your only originals. You can turn preparation off with IMAGE_OPTIMIZE_ENABLED=false or change the size with IMAGE_MAX_WIDTH and IMAGE_MAX_HEIGHT. Smart cropping, which looks for the interesting part of a picture, and a “museum” canvas-and-warmth effect are available but off by default.

3. It talks to the TV directly

Frame TV Art Manager connects to the TV over your home network using Samsung’s private Art Mode protocol. The first time, the TV shows an Allow/Deny prompt named “Frame Art Manager” (change it with CLIENT_NAME). Choose Allow once; the pairing token is saved in the data folder, so the TV doesn’t ask again. No Samsung account, SmartThings, or cloud service is involved.

4. It checks again every 30 minutes

The first sync runs as soon as the service starts. After that it repeats every SYNC_INTERVAL_MINUTES, 30 by default. Pictures that are already on the TV aren’t uploaded again. If the TV is off or unreachable, it simply tries again on the next sync.

How do I remove art from the TV?

Delete the picture from the artwork folder. On the next sync, Frame TV Art Manager removes the copy it uploaded from the TV. It only does this for pieces it uploaded itself, which it tracks for each TV in its data folder.

One exception keeps you from ending up with a blank collection by accident: if the folder becomes completely empty, the last piece it uploaded stays on the TV.

What happens when the TV fills up?

The Frame can only store so many pictures. When it refuses an upload as full, Frame TV Art Manager learns the limit and starts rotating: each piece it uploaded stays at least 14 days, then is swapped for art that’s waiting, never-shown pieces first. The storage-full and rotation guide explains the details and settings.

What won’t it do?

Frame TV Art Manager is careful about deleting things. Out of the box it will not:

  • Delete art you added yourself. Pieces you put on the TV through SmartThings or any other way stay, unless you deliberately set REMOVE_UNKNOWN_IMAGES=true.
  • Pick what’s on screen. It doesn’t change which piece the TV is displaying. If the piece on screen is one it removes, the TV shows another one.
  • Act on a TV it can’t read properly. If it can’t confirm what’s on the TV, or whether the TV is on and in Art Mode, it makes no changes that cycle.
  • Treat a hiccup as “delete everything”. A failed folder scan or a website that’s down never counts as an empty collection.
  • Change anything in a dry run. With DRY_RUN=true it reads your folder and the TV and plans, but uploads, deletes, and saves nothing. Dry run uses an existing pairing, so approve the TV once in normal mode first.

Can it sync more than one Samsung Frame TV?

Yes. List the addresses in TV_IPS, separated by commas, for example TV_IPS=192.168.1.150,192.168.1.151. Each TV gets the same collection, has its own pairing, and is tracked separately, so one TV being off doesn’t hold up the others. Each TV also learns its own storage limit for rotation.

Can it find art for me automatically?

Yes. Besides your own files, Frame TV Art Manager can download art from Unsplash, NASA (Astronomy Picture of the Day or a search), the Art Institute of Chicago, Pexels, Pixabay, and direct image links. Set ARTWORK_SOURCES_FILE=/data/sources.yaml and list what you want:

The Art Institute of Chicago and direct links need no key. NASA works with its shared demo key and accepts your own in NASA_API_KEY. Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay each need an API key from that service. These sources contact the internet; a folder-only setup doesn’t.

Downloaded pictures land in your artwork folder like any other file. If a source is down during a sync, the art it downloaded earlier stays. Removing a source from the list doesn’t delete what it already downloaded; delete those files yourself if you don’t want them.
