Free 4K art

Free public-domain 4K art for Samsung The Frame

Short answer: major museums publish high-resolution images of public-domain paintings for anyone to use. Frame TV Art Manager downloads them with no API key from the Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, and SMK (the National Gallery of Denmark), and from Wikimedia Commons' Picture of the Day and Featured Pictures. One line, museum:search:winter, searches all four museums at once and keeps only public-domain works. NASA, Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay work too, and every image is prepared for the TV's 3840×2160 screen.

Where to find free art for The Frame

Five sources are built in and need no account or API key. The others work through a direct image link or a free key.

"Public domain" means the copyright has expired; museums that release those images under CC0 put no conditions on them. Each site's terms are its own and can change, so check them before relying on an image. Note that NASA's APOD sometimes features images credited to other photographers, under their own terms.

Download art to The Frame automatically

Frame TV Art Manager can fetch art for you. Set ARTWORK_SOURCES_FILE=/data/sources.yaml; if the file doesn't exist, a commented starter file is created. A sources file looks like this:

providers:
  museum:
    - search:winter landscape   # up to 20 works mixed across four museums
  met:
    - search:sunflowers
    - object:436524             # one work by its ID
  cleveland:
    - search:snow?type=Painting # a search narrowed with a filter
  smk:
    - search:hammershøi
  art_institute_of_chicago:
    - photo:20684
  commons:
    - potd                      # Wikimedia Commons Picture of the Day
    - featured                  # the 10 newest Featured Pictures
  nasa:
    - apod                      # NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day
  direct:
    - https://example.com/painting.jpg

Downloads land in your artwork folder like any other picture, are prepared for the screen, and are uploaded on the next sync. If a source fails, nothing previously downloaded is removed and the rest of the sync goes ahead. Removing a line from the file doesn't delete what it already downloaded; delete those files yourself.

  • How much each line brings in: a museum search takes its first 10 results; museum:search takes up to 20, mixed across the museums.
  • Sized for 4K: images are requested just large enough to cover 3840×2160, with a smaller version as a fallback when the large one is over MAX_DOWNLOAD_SIZE_MB (20 MB by default) or 40 megapixels. Cleveland, SMK, and Commons images under 1920 pixels on the long edge are skipped.
  • Gentle on the museums: results are cached for 24 hours, rate limits are respected, and a provider that fails keeps its last good results.

A sources file adds art to your everyday collection, which shows all year. To show winter or holiday art only on certain dates, put it in a themed collection.

Search four museums by theme

museum:search:<theme> searches the Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, and SMK at once and mixes their results. When two museums hold copies of the same work by the same artist, common for prints, only one is kept. If one museum is down, the others' works are still used.

Public domain only. Searches of the Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, and SMK return only public-domain works, and Cleveland searches return only CC0 works. The public-domain and has-image filters are always applied, and unknown filters are rejected, so a typo can't widen a search.

Each museum can also be searched on its own, and The Met and Cleveland accept filters after a ?, written as name=value pairs joined by &:

  • The Met: departmentId, isHighlight, isOnView, artistOrCulture, title, tags, medium, geoLocation, dateBegin, dateEnd. For example, met:search:?isHighlight=true&departmentId=11; the query before the ? may be empty when a filter is given.
  • Cleveland: type (such as Painting or Print), department, highlight, artists, title, created_after, created_before, currently_on_view.
  • SMK takes no filters.

To add one work, use its ID from the museum's page: metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436524 is met:object:436524, clevelandart.org/art/1997.111 is cleveland:object:1997.111, open.smk.dk/artwork/image/KMS1 is smk:object:KMS1, and artic.edu/artworks/27992/… is art_institute_of_chicago:photo:27992.

Wikimedia Commons pictures and credit

Wikimedia Commons is a free media library whose community picks Featured Pictures and a Picture of the Day. commons:potd adds one picture a day (it changes at midnight UTC), commons:featured takes the 10 newest Featured Pictures, and commons:search:northern lights takes 10 Featured Pictures matching a query.

Commons files are often CC BY or CC BY-SA, which require crediting the author and license. Frame TV Art Manager records the title, artist, provider, source page, and license of every downloaded image under attributions in /status, for museum works too, and skips Commons files without license information.

What makes art look good on The Frame

  • Landscape works fill the screen best. Paintings close to 16:9, 3:2, or 4:3 are cropped only slightly; square and tall works are shown whole on a gallery mat.
  • Resolution matters. Aim for images at least 3840 pixels wide. Images that would need more than 3× enlargement are matted rather than blown up into blur.
  • Matte finish suits paintings. Oil paintings, watercolors, and prints look more like real art on The Frame's matte screen than glossy photographs do.
  • Change with the seasons. Searches such as museum:search:snow or museum:search:autumn give the collection a seasonal feel, and a themed collection shows holiday art only on the dates you choose.

A few public-domain favorites

  • Georges Seurat, A Sunday on La Grande Jatte, 1884 to 1886, Art Institute of Chicago.
  • Katsushika Hokusai, The Great Wave off Kanagawa, about 1831, in many museum collections.
  • Claude Monet's Water Lilies and Stacks of Wheat series.
  • Vincent van Gogh, The Bedroom, 1889, Art Institute of Chicago.
  • Pieter Bruegel the Elder, The Harvesters, 1565, The Met.
  • Vilhelm Hammershøi's quiet interiors, SMK.

Let your AI assistant choose the paintings

With the optional MCP endpoint turned on, an AI assistant such as Claude can search the same four museums for you (search_public_domain_art), add the works you like to a themed collection with their title, artist, license, and museum link, preview how each will be fitted to the screen, and schedule the collection. The manager holds no AI keys; see AI art for The Frame with MCP.

Questions people ask

Where can I get free art for my Samsung Frame TV?

Museum open-access programs at The Met, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK, the National Gallery of Art, the Rijksmuseum, and the Smithsonian publish public-domain artwork for free, and Wikimedia Commons curates Featured Pictures. NASA, Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay offer free images under their own terms.

Is public-domain art free to display on my TV?

Yes. Public-domain works are no longer under copyright, and museums that release images under CC0 put no conditions on using them. Wikimedia Commons files are often CC BY or CC BY-SA, which require crediting the author, and photo sites such as Unsplash use their own licenses, so check each one.

Can Frame TV Art Manager download art automatically?

Yes. List sources in a sources file and it downloads from the Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK, Wikimedia Commons, NASA, Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, and direct image links on each sync, then prepares and uploads the art.

Do I need an API key for museum art?

No. The Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK, and Wikimedia Commons need no key. Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay need a free key, and NASA works with its shared DEMO_KEY or a free key of your own.

Do the museum searches only return public-domain works?

Yes. Searches of the Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, and SMK return only public-domain works, and Cleveland searches return only CC0 works. The public-domain and has-image filters are always applied.

What resolution should free art be for The Frame?

At least 3840 pixels wide for the 4K models. Smaller images are shown whole on a gallery mat instead of being enlarged into blur.
