No subscription

Samsung Frame TV art without a subscription

Short answer: you don't need Samsung's Art Store to enjoy The Frame. Your own photos in My Photos are free to display, and museums publish thousands of public-domain paintings you can use at no cost. Frame TV Art Manager, a free self-hosted service, keeps a folder of that art on the TV for you: it prepares each picture for the 4K screen, uploads it over your home network, and swaps art in and out when the TV is full.

By MikeO7Published November 6, 2026Last updated November 7, 2026

This guide is for Frame owners who like Art Mode but don't want another monthly bill. It covers what Samsung charges for, what stays free, and how to get a steady supply of good-looking art onto the TV without paying for a subscription.

What costs money on The Frame, and what doesn't

The Samsung Art Store is a paid subscription: US$4.99 a month or US$49.99 a year as reported in October 2026, with prices varying by country. It unlocks Samsung's licensed catalog of paintings and photography.

Your own pictures are a different matter. Photos you add to the My Photos collection, from the SmartThings app, a USB stick, or a tool like Frame TV Art Manager, don't need a subscription to display. Samsung's own Art Mode page describes showing all of My Photos as free for personal photo collections.

About the slideshow reports. Since about July 2025, some owners have reported on Samsung's community forum that the "Display All" slideshow of My Photos asked them for an Art Store subscription. Samsung's help pages still describe it as free, and the behavior seems to vary by model and firmware. If your TV shows the prompt, the options below still work: they change which art is stored on the TV, and the TV can show any stored piece.

Three ways to fill Art Mode for free

1. Your own photos

Family photos, travel shots, and your kids' drawings all work. The catch with doing it by hand is the routine: one upload at a time from a phone, portraits that get cropped, and a TV that eventually runs out of room. Automatic uploads turn that into "drop files in a folder".

2. Public-domain masterpieces

The Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK, the National Gallery of Art, the Rijksmuseum, and the Smithsonian publish high-resolution images of public-domain works for anyone to use. Van Gogh, Monet, Hokusai, Vermeer, and Seurat are all there. Frame TV Art Manager downloads from the first four with no API key, for example museum:search:winter to search them all at once. The free 4K art guide lists where to look and how to pull art in automatically.

3. Free photo and space imagery

Wikimedia Commons' Picture of the Day and Featured Pictures, NASA's image library and Astronomy Picture of the Day, Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay offer free images under their own licenses. Frame TV Art Manager can download from all of them on a schedule; Wikimedia Commons needs no key, NASA works with its shared DEMO_KEY, and Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay need a free API key.

How to keep the art changing without a subscription

A Frame looks best when the art changes now and then. Frame TV Art Manager handles the parts that are tedious by hand:

  • New art arrives on its own. Pictures you add to the folder, and art from online sources, are uploaded on the next sync (every 30 minutes by default).
  • A large collection takes turns. When the TV is full, each piece the manager uploaded stays for 14 days (ROTATION_INTERVAL_DAYS) and is then swapped for art that hasn't been shown yet.
  • The slideshow can be set for you. SLIDESHOW_ENABLED, SLIDESHOW_INTERVAL (3, 15, 60, 720, or 1440 minutes), and SLIDESHOW_TYPE (shuffle or sequential) set the TV's slideshow for your uploaded photos over the local network. What the TV then allows is up to Samsung's firmware.
  • Nothing is uploaded twice. Pictures are recognized by their content, so re-adding the same file doesn't create duplicates on the TV.
  • Holiday art on its own schedule. A themed collection shows Christmas or Halloween art only on the dates you choose, then your everyday art returns.
  • Your AI assistant can help. With the optional MCP endpoint, an assistant such as Claude can make or find art and schedule it; see the AI art guide. The manager itself needs no AI subscription or key.

The manager never picks which piece is on screen; the TV and its slideshow do that. It also never deletes art you put on the TV yourself.

What you need

  • A Samsung Frame TV running Tizen 8.0 or newer. Older firmware may work, but Samsung's private Art Mode protocol changes between models and releases.
  • An always-on computer on the same network: a Synology, Unraid, or TrueNAS NAS, a mini PC, a Raspberry Pi-class board, or a Mac with Docker.
  • About ten minutes for the Docker setup. The only required setting is the TV's IP address.

No Samsung account, SmartThings account, or cloud service is involved; the service talks to the TV directly on your network.

What Frame TV Art Manager costs

Nothing for home use. Frame TV Art Manager is free for personal and other noncommercial use under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0; it is source-available, not open source. Commercial use, for example in a shop, office, or hotel, is not permitted. It is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Samsung.

Questions people ask

Do I need a subscription to show my own photos on The Frame?

No. Photos in My Photos can be displayed without the Art Store subscription. Some owners have reported since July 2025 that the Display All slideshow of My Photos asked for a subscription on their TVs; Samsung's help pages still describe it as free.

How much is the Samsung Art Store?

As reported in October 2026, US$4.99 per month or US$49.99 per year in the US. Prices vary by country, so check the Art Store on your TV.

Where can I get free art for a Samsung Frame TV?

Museum open-access collections such as the Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK, the National Gallery of Art, and the Rijksmuseum publish public-domain works for free. Wikimedia Commons, NASA, Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay offer free images under their own licenses. Frame TV Art Manager downloads from the first four museums and Wikimedia Commons with no API key.

Can Frame TV Art Manager unlock Art Store content?

No. It does not touch the Art Store or Samsung's licensed catalog. It only uploads your own pictures and art you choose from free sources.

Is Frame TV Art Manager really free?

Yes, for personal and other noncommercial use under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0. There is no paid tier and no account to create.
