For Samsung Frame TV owners with a home server

Keep your Samsung Frame TV art in sync with a folder.

Frame TV Art Manager is a self-hosted service that runs on your NAS, mini PC, or Mac. Put pictures in a folder and it prepares each one for the TV’s 4K screen, then uploads it to your Frame TVs over your home network, checking every 30 minutes. It never touches art you added yourself.

How it works

You manage a folder. Frame TV Art Manager handles the TV.

Uploading photos one at a time from a phone gets old quickly, especially with more than one TV. Frame TV Art Manager treats a folder as the list of what should be on your Frame TV and keeps the two matched.

01

Add pictures

Copy JPEG or PNG files into the artwork folder over a network share, send them from your phone with the optional web uploader, or pull art from sources like NASA and the Art Institute of Chicago.

02

It prepares each one

Sideways photos are turned upright, portraits are cropped or padded, colors are converted to sRGB, and each picture is resized to exactly 3840×2160, the Frame’s 4K resolution.

03

It keeps every TV matched

New pictures are uploaded, pictures you delete from the folder are removed from the TV, and if the TV runs out of room the art takes turns.

Features

Built to run unattended for months.

Frame TV Art Manager is meant to be set up once and then left alone. It keeps track of which pieces it put on each TV, so it can tidy up after itself without touching anything else.

TVs

One folder, every TV

List several addresses in TV_IPS. Each TV gets the same collection and is tracked separately, so one TV being off doesn’t hold up the others.

4K

Pictures that fit the screen

Upright orientation, portrait handling, sRGB color, HDR PNG tone mapping, light sharpening, and JPEG quality 95. A 24-megapixel photo takes about 1–2 seconds.

A shared folder, a password-protected upload page, iPhone Shortcuts, Unsplash, NASA, the Art Institute of Chicago, Pexels, Pixabay, or direct links. Exact duplicates are skipped.

LAN

No cloud account

It connects straight to the TV on your network. You approve it once on the TV, and nothing goes through Samsung’s servers.

Samsung mattes

Pick a matte for everything with MATTE_STYLE, such as shadowbox_polar, or set one per picture in a mattes.json file. Default is full-screen.

☼

Optional TV controls

Brightness that follows the sun, slideshow timing and shuffle, a nightly power-off for TVs left in Art Mode, and Wake-on-LAN. Each stays off until you set it.

✓

Easy to check on

/health and /status on port 8080 show whether the last sync worked and what each TV is doing. Logs are structured JSON.

Careful by default

What Frame TV Art Manager will and won’t do

Software that deletes things from your TV should be predictable. These are the rules it follows out of the box.

It will

  • Upload new pictures from your folder on the next sync.
  • Remove a piece it uploaded once you delete that picture from the folder.
  • Swap pieces it uploaded on a full TV, every 14 days by default.
  • Replace each picture in the artwork folder with its prepared 3840×2160 version, so keep your originals somewhere else.
  • Pick up where it left off after the TV was off or unreachable.

It won’t

  • Delete art you added yourself, unless you set REMOVE_UNKNOWN_IMAGES=true.
  • Choose which piece is on screen. That stays with the TV and its slideshow.
  • Empty the TV if the folder is emptied. It keeps the last piece it uploaded.
  • Drop downloaded art because a source website is down for a while.
  • Change anything on a TV whose state it can’t confirm, or anything at all with DRY_RUN=true.

Is it right for you?

A good fit if you already run something at home.

  • You have an always-on machine: Synology or another NAS, Unraid, a mini PC, a Raspberry Pi-class board, or a Mac.
  • Your Frame TV runs Tizen 8.0 or newer and is on the same network.
  • You change your art often, have a big collection, or have more than one Frame TV.
  • You’d rather drop files in a folder than repeat uploads from a phone.

Know before you install

When something else is better

If you only add a photo now and then and don’t run a home server, Samsung’s SmartThings app is simpler. Compare the two.

Frame TV Art Manager uses Samsung’s private Art Mode protocol. It targets Tizen 8.0 and newer; other firmware may work, but that protocol varies between models and updates.

Install

One setting to get started.

The only required setting is TV_IPS. Everything else has a sensible default.

  1. Give the TV a fixed IP address with a DHCP reservation in your router.
  2. Save this compose.yaml with your TV’s address and run docker compose up -d.
  3. With the TV on, choose Allow when “Frame Art Manager” asks to connect. You only do this once.
  4. Copy JPEG or PNG files into data/artwork. They reach the TV on the next sync.

FAQ

Common questions

What is Frame TV Art Manager?

Frame TV Art Manager is a self-hosted service for Samsung Frame TV owners who run a NAS or home server. It keeps the TV’s art in sync with a folder of JPEG and PNG images: it prepares each picture for the 3840×2160 screen and uploads it over your home network, checking for changes every 30 minutes.

Can it automatically upload art to a Samsung Frame TV?

Yes. Add a picture to the artwork folder and Frame TV Art Manager uploads it on the next sync, which runs at startup and then every 30 minutes by default (SYNC_INTERVAL_MINUTES). Remove a picture from the folder and the copy it uploaded is removed from the TV. Pictures that have not changed are not uploaded again.

Do I need SmartThings or a Samsung account?

No. Frame TV Art Manager talks to the TV directly on your local network, so a folder-based setup needs no SmartThings app, Samsung account, or cloud service. Optional online art sources such as Unsplash or NASA contact those services only when you turn them on.

Will it delete art I already put on my TV?

Not by default. Frame TV Art Manager only removes art it uploaded itself. Art you added another way, for example with the SmartThings app, stays on the TV unless you deliberately set REMOVE_UNKNOWN_IMAGES=true.

What happens when my Frame TV's storage is full?

Frame TV Art Manager rotates your art. The first time the TV refuses an upload because it is full, it learns how many pieces the TV holds. From then on, each piece it uploaded stays on the TV for at least 14 days (ROTATION_INTERVAL_DAYS) and is then swapped for waiting art, never-shown pieces first, so a large collection takes turns. Art you added yourself is never rotated out. Read the rotation guide.

Will it change the artwork showing on my TV?

Frame TV Art Manager does not choose which artwork is on screen; that stays with the TV and its slideshow settings. If a piece it manages is removed or rotated out while it is showing, the TV moves on to another piece.

How does it handle portrait photos?

Portrait photos are cropped to fill the 16:9 screen by default (PORTRAIT_MODE=crop). You can instead place them over a blurred background (pad) or pair two portraits side by side (collage). Every picture is resized to exactly 3840×2160 unless you choose a different size.

What hardware can I run it on?

Any always-on machine on the same network as the TV that can run Docker: a Synology or other NAS, an Unraid server, a mini PC, a Raspberry Pi-class Linux board, or a Mac. The container image is built for amd64, arm64, and armv7, and you can also build a single Go binary.

Which Frame TVs does it work with?

Frame TV Art Manager targets Samsung Frame TVs running Tizen 8.0 or newer. Older firmware may work, but Samsung’s private Art Mode protocol changes between models and releases, so it is not guaranteed. You can manage several TVs at once by listing their addresses in TV_IPS.

Is Frame TV Art Manager free and open source?

Frame TV Art Manager is free for personal and other noncommercial use under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0. It is source-available, not open source: the code is public, but commercial use is not permitted. It is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Samsung.

Ready to try it?

Install it, approve it once, add a few pictures.

Frame TV Art Manager runs its first sync as soon as it starts, so uploads begin as soon as you approve the connection on the TV. After that, it keeps the TV in sync on its own.
